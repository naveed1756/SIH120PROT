## 1. Goal

### 1.1 What this is

A digital twin that jointly optimises Cyclic Steam Stimulation (CSS) and sucker-rod pumping (SRP) on heavy-oil wells of the Baghewala field (Rajasthan, Jodhpur Sandstone, crude of 14–17° API, ~11,500 cP at 50 °C). The full architecture is in `docs/`. **You are not building the full system.**

### 1.2 What you are building

A thin vertical slice of physics and ML running on one **synthetic** well, BGW-SYN-01. Its only purpose is to produce **visual assets** (PNG stills, MP4 animations, and a web dashboard with a 3D well) for a 6-slide idea PPT.

The assets must convince an Oil India engineer, in about 10 seconds each, of three things:

1. The team can actually simulate the well: reservoir heat, wellbore, rods and pump.
2. The team understood the real problem. As the steam-heated zone cools, oil viscosity in the tubing rises and the rods start to "float" (they cannot fall as fast as the pumping unit pulls them down). This coupling from CSS to SRP is what the problem statement calls "well-to-surface".
3. There is AI on top that fills a real data gap. It learns from simulated failure cards, forecasts rod float, and recommends pump speed (SPM).

### 1.3 Success

Success means the asset list in §6 exists in `assets/`, every figure is physically consistent with the equations in §5, and every figure carries the synthetic-data caption (§3.2).

Pretty-but-wrong is a failure. Correct-but-plain is acceptable.

## 4. Domain primer

Read this so the equations in §5 make sense.

- **CSS cycle.**
  - *Injection:* ~3.1 t/h of wet steam for 14–21 days at 85–97 ksc.
  - *Soak:* the well is shut in for about 55% of the injection time while heat conducts outward.
  - *Pumped production:* a sucker-rod pump lifts oil as the heated zone ("halo") around the well cools and shrinks.
  - Then the cycle repeats.
- **Heat-loss path.** Heat escapes up and down into the cap and base rock. Steam rises to the top of the pay ("override"), so the hot zone is wedge-shaped, wider at the top.
- **Viscosity.** It falls steeply with temperature: ~11,500 cP at 50 °C, ~300 cP at 100 °C, ~11 cP at 200 °C.
- **Wellbore cooling.** At this field's low rates (~5 m³/d), fluid entering the pump at 150 °C cools to ~40 °C by the wellhead even through vacuum-insulated tubing (VIT). So the viscosity the rods see is set by the tubing, not the reservoir.
- **Rod string.** About 1,100 m of steel rod moving up and down inside the tubing, behaving as an elastic spring (a damped wave equation).
- **Dynamometer card.** A plot of polished-rod load against position over one stroke. Its shape diagnoses the pump: full pump, fluid pound, gas interference, valve leaks, and so on. The *surface card* is measured at the top of the rods. The *downhole card* is computed from it (the Gibbs method).
- **Rod float.** On the downstroke the rods must fall through viscous oil. If the carrier bar moves down faster than the rods can fall, the rods separate from it: load goes to zero and there is impact on the next upstroke. Remedies are slowing the pump (lower SPM), slowing only the downstroke, heating the tubing, or adding diluent.
- **Water cut.** Early in production the fluid is mostly condensed steam water. A water-continuous emulsion is thin. As water cut falls below the inversion point (~60%), the mixture becomes oil-continuous and thick. Combined with tubing cooling, this is what triggers rod float mid-cycle.

### T03: `twin/thermal_rz.py` (T1-A, simplified) · Must

**Read:** 3A §5.1–5.6. This brief simplifies the doc model as follows:
- one pressure per phase, so T_sat is constant within a phase;
- energy equation only, with no water-mass equation;
- vertical transport only by conduction; override enters only through the injection allocation.

**Grid:**
- `Nr = 40` cell edges geometric from `RW_M` to `RE_M`.
- Z: cap 12 cells (geometric 0.25 m → 60 m, fine near the pay), pay 10 × 1 m (layers 4/3/3), base 12 cells mirrored.
- Axisymmetric finite-volume geometry: face areas `2π r h` and `π(r²₊ − r²₋)`, volumes `π(r²₊ − r²₋)Δz`.

**State:** volumetric enthalpy `H` per cell (J/m³ above T_R reference). Mapping H → T with the phase pressure `p`:
- `H_l(p)` is the enthalpy of the cell with all pore water liquid at T_sat(p).
- If `H ≤ H_l`: solve T from `H = M_eff·(T − T_R)`. Pay: `M_eff = M_R` (the 2.4 MJ/m³K includes pore fluid). Cap/base: `M_OB`.
- Else: `T = T_sat(p)`, and the excess `(H − H_l)` is latent steam. Store `S_s = (H − H_l)/(PHI·ρ_s·L_v)`, capped at 1.

**Equation** (A.1, backward Euler in time):

```
V·(H^{n+1}−H^n)/Δt = Σ_faces λ_face·A·ΔT/Δx  −  Σ_radial_faces F_adv  +  S
```

- `λ`: geometric mean of rock and fluid in the pay; `LAM_OB` in cap/base; harmonic mean at faces.
- Advection only on radial faces inside the pay, upwind: `F_adv = ρ_w·u·A·h_w(T_upwind)`, with `h_w` measured relative to `h_w(T_R)` using iapws liquid enthalpy.
- Linearise T(H) with Picard iterations (2–4) and assemble a sparse matrix; solve with `scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve` or `splu`.
- If the implicit version fails after two attempts, fall back to explicit sub-stepping under a CFL limit inside each hour. Record this in `PROGRESS.md`.

**Phases:**

| Phase | Pressure | Radial velocity | Heat input |
|---|---|---|---|
| Injection | p_bh ≈ 99 ksc(g) | `u_k(r) = ṁ_k/(2π r h_k ρ_w)` | At r_w per layer: `q_k = w_k·ṁ·(h_w(T_sat) + X_SF·L_v − h_w(T_R))` |
| Soak | P_NEARWELL_KSC | `u = 0` | none |
| Production | P_NEARWELL_KSC | `u < 0`, from layer rates given by T04 | energy leaving at r_w is logged as `E_prod` |

- Injection allocation: `w_k ∝ KH_k·exp(BETA·ζ_k)`, where ζ_k is layer mid-height / pay height (0 = bottom, 1 = top). Total heat ≈ 1.6 MW.
- Time step: Δt = 3600 s, and 300 s for the first 6 h after each phase change.
- Boundaries: T_R fixed at r_e, top and bottom (geotherm variation across 120 m is ignored).

**Outputs** (`run_cycle()` returns a dict; also saved to `out/thermal.npz`):
- `T[t, z, r]` every 6 h
- per-layer `r_h` (outermost r with T − T_R ≥ 5 K) hourly
- `T_in` (flow-weighted T of the innermost pay cells; during injection report T_sat)
- `T_bar_heated` per layer (pore-volume-weighted T inside r_h)
- `E_res`
- heat budget hourly: `E_sf`, `ΔE_pay`, `ΔE_cap`, `ΔE_base`, `E_prod`, `eps_rel`

**Done when:**
1. `|eps_rel| < 1%` at every step.
2. Marx–Langenheim check: `BETA=0`, single 10 m layer, 14 d / 21 d injection, M_R = 2.4e6, K_ob = 1.7, M_ob = 2.3e6, ΔT = 253 K. The heated area (r_h = radius where T − T_R ≥ ½·ΔT) must be within 20% of **276 / 403 m²** (radius 9.4 / 11.3 m). Also implement the analytical `G(t_D)` from eq. A.7–A.8 and compare over time.

4. Conduction-only unit test: 1-D slab (set `Nr` = 1-like) against the erf solution, within 3%.

### T06: `twin/kinematics.py` + `twin/rodpump.py` (T2-B predictive) · Must

**Read:** Layer 2 §4 (T2-B box). The equations below are complete for the PoC.

**Kinematics** (`y` = polished-rod position, positive up, 0 at bottom):
- **Beam:** `y = S/2·(1 − cos(2πNt/60))`.
- **Hydraulic:** trapezoidal velocity profile with acceleration 0.5 m/s², separate `v_up` and `v_down`, and period `60/N`. Solve for plateau speeds given N and S. With `down_fraction > 0.5` the downstroke is slower.

**Rod string** (x downward from the polished rod; u = downward displacement):
- Equation: `u_tt = a²·u_xx − c(x)·u_t + g_b`
  - `a = √(E_STEEL/RHO_STEEL)` ≈ 5,135 m/s
  - `g_b = g·(1 − ρ_f/ρ_s)`
  - `c(x) = K_C_DRAG·2π·μ_mix(x)/(RHO_STEEL·A_r·ln(TUB_RI_M/ROD_R_M))`
- Explicit central difference, Δx = 10 m, Δt = 1.5 ms (assert `a·Δt/Δx ≤ 1`):
  `u_i^{n+1} = [r²(u_{i+1} − 2u_i + u_{i−1}) + 2u_i − (1 − c_iΔt/2)·u_i^{n−1} + Δt²·g_b] / (1 + c_iΔt/2)`
- **Top BC:** `u_0 = −y(t)`.
- **Bottom BC** via ghost node: `u_{N+1} = u_{N−1} + 2Δx·F_p/(E·A_r)`, where F_p (tension, positive) comes from `pumpbc.py`.
- **Initial condition:** static equilibrium under gravity and F_p at the start of the upstroke. Run 3 strokes and keep the 3rd.
- **Polished-rod load:** `F_pr = E·A_r·(u_1 − u_0)/Δx`, plus the rod's own static offset if it is not captured. Verify with the static test below.
- **Carrier-bar separation:** if `F_pr < 0`, set the reported load to 0 and flag the stroke as `float`.
- **Downhole card:** position `−u_N` against load `F_p`.

**Done when:**
1. Static test: with u fixed (no motion) and F_p = F_fl, F_pr = buoyant rod weight + F_fl within 1%. Buoyant weight ≈ 26.2 N/m × 1,100 m ≈ 28.8 kN.

### T07: `twin/pumpbc.py` + `ml/card_library.py` (D1 / I6) · Must

**Pump boundary condition.** A state machine on the plunger's direction and travel within the stroke. S_p is the plunger stroke taken from the previous cycle. `F_fl = (p_dis − p_int)·A_p`, with `p_dis` from the fluid column (~ρ_f·g·ROD_LEN) and `p_int` = 5–40 ksc.

| Class id | Rule |
|---|---|
| `normal` | upstroke F_fl, downstroke 0, linear ramps over 3% of S_p |
| `fluid_pound` | downstroke holds F_fl for (1−f)·S_p, f ∈ [0.4, 0.8], then drops over 1% of S_p |
| `steam_gas_interference` | same travel as fluid pound, but a gradual convex (polytropic, n = 1.2) drop over (1−f)·S_p |
| `rod_float` | normal pump; μ_mix uniform or from a T05 profile with an effective 5–40 Pa·s, so clipping occurs |
| `tv_leak` | upstroke `F_fl·(1 − k·τ)`, τ = normalised upstroke time, k ∈ [0.2, 0.6] |
| `sv_leak` | downstroke starts at `α·F_fl` (α ∈ [0.2, 0.5]) and decays linearly to 0 |
| `unseated` | F_p = 0.05·F_fl throughout |
| `rod_parted` | rod length = break depth ∈ [0.3, 0.9]·ROD_LEN; F_p = 0 at the break |
| `tagging` | normal, plus a −0.3·F_fl impulse lasting 30 ms at the bottom of the downstroke |

**Library generation:**
- 9 classes × 1,500 cards, seed 42.
- Sample per card:
  - unit: 80% beam, 20% hydraulic
  - N: 2–8 SPM
  - S: 2.5–4 m (hydraulic 4–7 m)
  - pump depth: 900–1,150 m
  - μ_mix: log-uniform 0.05–40 Pa·s (non-float classes are capped at ~3 Pa·s unless float co-occurs; keep labels single-class and drop cards whose float flag contradicts a non-float label)
  - p_int: 5–40 ksc
  - class severities as in the table
- Add 2–5% Gaussian load noise and ±1% position jitter.
- Resample each surface and downhole card to 200 points.
- Save to `out/cards.parquet`: parameters, class, flags, arrays.
- Vectorise across cards where possible. Expected generation time is minutes, not hours. If it is slower, reduce to 800 per class and note it.

**Done when:** every class produces at least 90% valid cards (closed, finite), and each class's median card is visibly distinct in a quick-look plot.

**Asset** `assets/A3_card_library.png`: a 3×3 grid, one representative card per class. Surface card in wellbore blue, downhole card dashed grey, class name as the panel title, and a one-line physical cause under each (e.g. "incomplete pump fillage"). Load axis in kN, position in m.

### T08: `twin/gibbs.py`: diagnostic mode and round-trip · Should

- Implement the surface → downhole transform: take surface position and load, solve the same damped wave equation marching in space from x = 0 (the known displacement and force at the top) down to the pump. Use the finite-difference-in-space scheme (Everitt–Jennings) with the same c(x).
- **Round-trip test:** simulate a surface card from a known pump BC (normal, and fluid pound), invert it, and compare the recovered downhole card with the imposed F_p against plunger position.
- **Done when:** RMS error ≤ 5% of the load range for both cases at μ ≤ 1 Pa·s. Report the error at 10 Pa·s too, even if it is larger. This is informative, not a failure.
- **Asset** `assets/A6b_gibbs_roundtrip.png`: surface card, imposed pump card and recovered pump card overlaid, with the RMS error in the legend.

### T09: `twin/float_glide.py` + the coupling chart (F / O2 lite) · Must

This is the most important asset. Take care over it.

**Float margin:**
- Terminal rod fall speed: `v_fall(z) = (ρ_s − ρ_f)·g·A_r·ln(r_ti/r_r)/(K_C_DRAG·2π·μ_mix(z))`, which is ≈ 5.13/(K_C_DRAG·μ[Pa·s]) m/s for these rods.
- Effective value over the string: length-weighted **harmonic** mean.
- Maximum downstroke speed: `v_down_max = π·S·N/60` (beam) or the hydraulic plateau `v_down`.
- `float_margin = 1 − v_down_max/v_fall_eff`.

**Pump sizing:** choose `PLUNGER_D_M` from standard sizes (1.25, 1.5, 1.75, 2.0, 2.25 in) so that `N_inflow = q_liq/(A_p·S·0.8·1440)` is 5–6 SPM at the production peak. Write it to `params.py`.

**Glide path:**
- `N_float(t) = 0.9·60·v_fall_eff(t)/(π·S)`
- `N_glide(t) = min(N_inflow(t), 0.9·N_float(t))`
- Baseline "current practice" = N held constant at the peak `N_inflow`.

**Float onset forecast:** the first t at which baseline float_margin < 0. Pick a demo "now" 14 h before onset; the recommendation fires there.

**What-ifs:**
- (a) 6 kW heater → new onset day.
- (b) hydraulic unit, same stroke volume per minute, S = 6 m, down_fraction 0.6 → new onset day.

**Cross-check with T06:** run the wave equation at three production times (well before onset, at onset, after onset) using the T05 μ_mix(z) profile and baseline N. Min polished-rod load must be > 0, ≈ 0 (clipping starts) and clipped, respectively. If the analytic onset and wave-equation onset disagree by more than 3 days, report both. Do not hide the discrepancy.

**Tuning knobs**, in this order, only if onset is outside production days 30–80:
1. `T_PROD_D` and the decline of q_liq (via water-cut constants);
2. `F_INV`;
3. heater baseline.

Record the final values.

## 8. When to stop and ask the human

Stop, write the issue in `PROGRESS.md` under "Blocked", and ask when any of these happen:

- `docs/` is missing, or a doc section referenced here does not exist.
- A done-when test still fails after **two** honest attempts. Report the failing value, what you tried, and your best hypothesis. Never relax the threshold on your own.
- A tuning knob cannot reach its target within physically plausible bounds:
  - K_MD above 5,000 mD;
  - no float onset between days 10 and 150 with any listed knob.
- A required package cannot be installed.
- You are about to change a data contract in T12, a parameter's evidence label, or anything in the "Do NOT build" list (§3.1).

For anything else, make the most reasonable choice, record it under "Decisions" in `PROGRESS.md`, and continue.
