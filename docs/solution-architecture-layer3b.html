3Where these modules sit

T2-A is the only path between the steam generator and the reservoir, and between the reservoir and the pump. In injection it tells T1-A how much heat actually arrived. In production it tells T2-B how thick the oil is along the rods. O3 sits above everything: it asks T1-D thousands of "what if" questions, checks the best answers on the full twin with O2 running the pump, and hands one steam request to O5.

4.1Components

W1Completion and resistance builder

Builds the depth segments and the radial heat-loss path for the string in use in each phase (VIT or bare tubing, N2 annulus, casing, cement).

W2Formation memory

A small radial conduction model around each segment that keeps the rock's heat from one phase to the next. Hasan–Kabir is its fallback and check.

W3Injection solver

Marches steam down the well: energy, momentum and IAPWS-IF97 properties give quality, pressure and heat loss.

W4Production solver

Marches oil and water up the rod–tubing annulus: non-Newtonian friction averaged over the stroke, heater, diluent and viscous heating.

W5 · W6Flowback and shut-in

Flash-assisted natural flow with a TPR curve; cooling of a stagnant column, below-pour-point interval and restart risk.

W7 · W8Integrity and intake

Casing temperature, wellhead growth, N2 annulus pressure; intake conditions, flash margin and the intake-pressure soft sensor.

4.2Heat-loss path from fluid to rock

q′ = (Tf − Th) / Rw, Rw = 1/(2πrtihti) + ln(rins,o/rins,i)/(2πkVITccpl) + 1/(2πrto(hc+hr)) + ln(rwb/rco)/(2πkcem) + ΣRsteel(W.1) wellbore resistance
hr = σ (T12 + T22)(T1 + T2) / [ 1/εto + (rto/rci)(1/εci − 1) ](W.2) annulus radiation, T in K
khc/kha = 0.049 (Gr·Pr)1/3 Pr0.074, hc = khc / (rto ln(rci/rto)), Gr = g β ΔT δ3ρ2/μ2(W.3) Dropkin–Somerscales

Th: temperature at the cement–formation boundary (from W2). kVIT: apparent conductivity of the vacuum layer. ccpl ≥ 1: coupling factor for the uninsulated joints every ~9.6 m, calibrated (prior 1.2–1.4). ε ≈ 0.9 for oxidised steel. N2 properties at the annulus mean temperature and fill pressure; δ = rci − rto. The two annulus terms depend on the surface temperatures T1 (outer tube) and T2 (casing), so each segment iterates the radial temperatures, typically in 5–10 passes.

4.3Formation memory

Layer 2 names Hasan–Kabir's transient solution. It assumes constant conditions since the well started, which is never true across injection, soak, flowback and pumping. W2 therefore gives each depth segment its own small radial conduction model in the rock, carried across phases and cycles. The rock stays warm after 18 days of steam, and that warmth lowers heat loss early in production. The analytical form is kept for testing and as the fallback.

(ρc)e ∂T/∂t = (1/r) ∂/∂r ( ke r ∂T/∂r ), −2πrwbke ∂T/∂r|rwb = q′, T(30 m) = Tei(z)(W.4) per segment
TD = ln[ e−0.2tD + (1.5 − 0.3719 e−tD) √tD ], tD = αet/rwb2, Rform = TD/(2πke)(W.5) Hasan–Kabir check

Grid: 25 log-spaced cells from rwb to 30 m (penetration √(4αt) is ~10 m after a year); implicit in time. 115 segments × 25 cells ≈ 2,900 unknowns, solved as independent tridiagonal systems in well under a millisecond each. Test: with constant q′ from t = 0, W.4 must reproduce W.5 within 3%.

4.4Injection mode: steam down the well

dh/dz = g cosθ − q′/w − v dv/dz(W.6) energy, z downward
dp/dz = ρm g cosθ − f ρm v2/(2D) − ρm v dv/dz(W.7) momentum
x = (h − hl(p)) / Lv(p), Tf = Tsat(p) while 0 < x < 1(W.8) state, IAPWS-IF97

h: mixture specific enthalpy. ρm and f come from a two-phase correlation that handles downward flow: Beggs–Brill is primary; the homogeneous no-slip model is the check. The choice is settled against any bottomhole P/T survey OIL has. The same equations run first over the surface steam line from generator to wellhead (length and insulation are master data), so the model starts from the generator's measured outlet, not an assumed wellhead state. The boundary condition is measured pressure and quality; temperature is Tsat(p). A measured steam temperature below Tsat at its own pressure (as OIL's 280 °C is at 85 ksc(g)) is flagged as an instrument or location issue, not treated as subcooled steam (Part A §2). Segments are 10 m (finer in the top 50 m); each marches with Heun's method and iterates W.1–W.4.

4.5Production mode: oil up the rod annulus

In pumped production, fluid rises in the annulus between the rods and the tubing, pushed in pulses by the pump. Three things differ from injection: the flow is laminar and non-Newtonian; the inner film resistance is large; and heaters, diluent and viscous heating add or move heat.

w cp dT/dz↑ = −q′ + q′heat(z) + Q |dp/dz|fric(W.9) energy, upward; last term is viscous heating
dp/dz↑ = −ρmix g cosθ − (1/Ts) ∫stroke Φ(v(t)) dt(W.10) momentum, stroke-averaged friction
Φ(v̄) = K [ (2n+1)/n · v̄ ]n (2/δ)n+1, Newtonian limit 12μ v̄/δ2(W.11) power-law slot, gap δ = rti − rrod
hti = Nu · ko/Dh · (μb/μw)0.14, Nu ≈ 4.36 (laminar), Dh = 2(rti − rrod)(W.12) film, Sieder–Tate correction

v(t): annulus velocity through one stroke, from T2-B's pump discharge (fluid moves mostly on the upstroke). Averaging friction over the stroke, instead of using the daily mean rate, matters because friction is non-linear in v for shear-thinning oil. K, n and any yield stress come from T1-B in mixture mode, using water holdup, not water cut. The drag the moving rods feel is T2-B's; T2-A only uses the fluid pressure. Heater: q′heat = P/L over the heater interval (a cable heater) or a point source at the pump. Diluent: injected at depth zd; above it, enthalpy is mixed and viscosity blended by Refutas (T1-B B.9).

Water holdup in the rod annulus (the Hw that T1-B's mixture mode needs). Default: no slip, Hw = water cut. The pump shears oil and water into a water-in-oil dispersion, the density contrast is small (heavy oil ~940–960 kg/m³ against water ~990–1,000 kg/m³ at tubing temperatures), and the continuous oil phase is viscous, so drops barely slip. Above the inversion water fraction (T1-B B.8), or once the stroke-averaged velocity falls below a few mm/s during slow pumping or shut-in, the drift-flux form Hw = fw/(C0 + vd/vm) is used, with C0 ≈ 1.0–1.2 and drift velocity vd from Stokes settling of water drops in the local oil viscosity. Both are closures: the friction multiplier (4.9) absorbs holdup error, and THP against the intake-pressure soft sensor is the calibration signal.

Closed-form check (Ramey, upward flow)

Tf(ℓ) = Tei(ℓ) + (Tin − Tbh) e−ℓ/LR + gG LR (1 − e−ℓ/LR), LR = w cp Rtotal(W.13)

ℓ: distance up from the pump; gG: geothermal gradient; LR: relaxation length, the distance over which the fluid "forgets" its inlet temperature. This is exact for constant properties and no heater, and is used as the unit test for W4.

Pump intake and flash margin

  • Intake conditions: Tint from the production march at pump depth; pint from the coupling solve (IPR meets pump delivery).
  • Intake-pressure soft sensor New: pint = pdis − Ffluid/Ap. Here pdis comes from marching down from measured THP (W.10), and Ffluid is the fluid load on the plunger from T2-B's downhole card. If the N2 annulus is packed off in production, acoustic fluid levels are impossible, and this becomes the main intake-pressure measurement (question for OIL).
  • Flash margin: ΔTsub = Tsat(pint) − Tint. Below 10 °C is a warning; below 3 °C, D1 is told to expect steam interference rather than fluid pound, and O2 is told to raise intake pressure.

4.6Flowback mode: why wells flow after CSS

OIL reports wells flowing unaided for weeks after CSS. Two causes are modelled together. The heated zone is still above its original pressure (T1-C's ph). And as hot water rises and the pressure falls below Tsat, part of it flashes to steam, which lightens the column like gas lift.

xw(z) = max(0, (hw − hl(p)) / Lv(p) ), ρmix, f from Beggs–Brill (oil + water + steam)(W.14) flashing
TPR(q) = pwf required to deliver q at flowline pressure pfl; natural-flow point: IPR(pwf) = TPR−1(W.15) nodal, with T1-C

T2-A publishes the TPR curve (20 rates) every hour; T1-C finds the intersection. End of natural flow: F rolls T1 forward (cooling, falling ph) and reports the first day the curves stop meeting. That date is when lift must be ready, and O2 plans initial settings for it.

4.7Soak and shut-in: cooling and restart risk

With no flow, each segment's fluid column cools into the casing and rock (W.1 inner resistances + W.4). T2-A calculates the temperature profile over time. What that temperature means for restarting depends on four different things, which are not interchangeable:

Outputs. The depth interval where the calculated T is below Tpp, how long the pump depth has been below it, and a restart-risk category:

  • Low: the whole column stays above Tpp + 10 °C.
  • Medium: part of the tubing is below Tpp + 10 °C, but the pump depth is not below Tpp.
  • High: the pump depth, or more than a set length of tubing (proposed 100 m), has been below Tpp for longer than a set time (proposed 6 h).

The +10 °C margin, the 100 m length and the 6 h duration are provisional engineering screening thresholds, not supported by Baghewala field or lab data. They will be replaced once gel-strength tests (Part A 6.5) and restart history are available. Until gel-strength data exists, T2-A does not predict a restart pressure or a time to gel. Below T1-B's validity envelope (Tpp + 3 °C) T2-A calls T1-B with allow_gel and receives the capped, flagged gel regime (Part A 6.4). It uses only the temperature and the category from it, never a load. H's restart procedure uses the category: at high risk, heat first (heater or hot-oil circulation), then ramp SPM from the minimum with the most conservative float margin.

4.8Well integrity: wellhead growth and annulus pressure

ΔLwh(t) ≈ αs ∫0Lfree [ Tcas(z,t) − Tcas,0(z) ] dz, αs ≈ 1.2×10−5 K−1(W.16) wellhead growth
pann(t) ≈ pann,0 · ZT̄ann(t) / (Z0T̄ann,0), leak index = (pmeas − pann) / pann(W.17) sealed N2 annulus, T in K

Lfree: effective length of casing free to expand (poor cement bond or uncemented section). It is unknown, so it is calibrated from measured wellhead growth (Layer 2 data item 33). With grade D VIT the top-100 m casing warms by only ~10–30 °C; with bare tubing it warms by ~200 °C. At 100 m of free casing that is ~25 cm of growth, the scale of the 2006 failure. W.17 ignores volume change from tubing and casing expansion, which is a small correction. A leak index below −10% that persists for 6 h raises an alarm. A rising kVIT together with higher annulus pressure points to VIT vacuum loss rather than a leak.

What O3 uses: the time history of growth and annulus pressure under a candidate ramp-up. The first hours of injection are when the casing heats fastest. This is why O3 treats the ramp-up profile as a design variable (5.3).

By phase

  1. Before injection: O3 calls T2-A on candidate ramp-ups to predict casing temperature, growth and annulus pressure.
  2. Injection (every 15 min): read the generator outlet, march the surface line and the well, and send sandface heat to T1-A. Predict annulus pressure and growth and compare with measurements; the leak index goes to D4. Every 6 h, send the delivered-heat track to O3's re-planner.
  3. Soak (hourly): cooling and below-pour-point interval; the wellhead pressure prediction for SE's fall-off assimilation.
  4. Flowback (hourly): TPR to T1-C; natural-flow point; end-of-flow forecast via F.
  5. Pumped (15 min, and on every coupling solve): T, μ, ρ profile to T2-B; intake conditions; soft-sensor intake pressure to SE; wellhead T to SE.

Validation

Coupling rules

  • Injection. The measured generator rate and pressure are T2-A's boundary conditions; it marches down and computes pbh. T1-C, using ph from the last hourly step (one-step lag), gives the rate the reservoir would accept at that pbh. The two are not forced to agree: the mismatch is passed to SE as an observation of injectivity and ph. Heat delivered in the 15-min T2-A steps is summed and handed to T1-A at its hourly step, so no energy is lost between cadences.
  • Pumped production. Tin comes from T1-A and is held (lag ≤ 1 h). Within each coupling solve, T2-A and the solve iterate (intake pressure ↔ profile), up to three times. T2-B uses the latest profile until the next update.
  • Flowback. T2-A's TPR and T1-C's IPR are solved together each hour by Brent's method.
  • Soak and shut-in. No flow. T2-A cools the column; its wellhead-pressure prediction uses T1-C's hourly ph.

Immediately after a phase change

  1. T2-A switches mode but keeps the formation memory: the rock around the well is never reset.
  2. For 6 h, T2-A runs 1-min substeps and T1 runs 5-min steps, because this is when the wellbore and casing heat up or cool fastest.
  3. O2 and O3 re-plans wait for 2 h of post-change data, unless an integrity trigger fires (growth or annulus pressure near a limit).
  4. SE pauses slow-parameter updates for 6 h (the transient is not yet informative for them); fast states keep updating.

What triggers a finer time step

A phase change; a measured rate change above 20% within 15 min; generator trip or start; heater or diluent switched on or off; a temperature change above 5 °C in one step at any segment; a wellhead-pressure change above 5 ksc in one step; energy closure above 0.5%. All thresholds are provisional.

5.3Objectives

OIL's prices and costs are not available yet (registry P-41). O3 therefore does not claim an economic optimum. It works in two phases.

Now: normalised physical objectives

J1 = max Np / (tinj + tsoak + L + tmob) [bbl per cycle-day](O.0a) oil per day of the well's time
J2 = min SOR = Ms/Np; J3 = min lift risk (float days + D3 failure risk); J4 (optional) = min lift energy per bbl(O.0b)

Diesel, water and CO2 per barrel are fixed multiples of SOR (0.071 t diesel, 1.77 t water, 0.225 t CO2 per tonne of steam, registry P-40), so minimising SOR already minimises them. That keeps the objective count at three.

When OIL economics arrive: value per cycle-day

J1 = max V / (tinj + tsoak + L + tmob)(O.1) value per cycle-day
V = Σt [ Po qo(t) − ce Elift(t) − Cheat,dil(t) − Cfix ] − Csteam − Crisk(O.2) cycle value
Csteam = Ms ( 0.071 cHSD + 1.77 cwater + cgen ) + Cmob, Crisk = Σ pfail · Cworkover(O.3) costs per tonne of steam
J2 = min SOR = Ms/Np; J3 = min lift risk (float days + D3 failure risk); J4 = min CO2/bbl = (0.225 Ms + egridElift)/Np(O.4) other objectives

Dividing by the whole cycle length (downtime included) keeps O3 consistent with O4's cut-off rule: both maximise value per day of the well's time, so a longer cycle has to earn its extra days. qo(t) and Elift(t) come from runs with O2 operating the pump, so a design that floats rods early or needs high lift energy is scored for it (Layer 2 I3). 0.071 t HSD, 1.77 t water and 0.225 t CO2 per tonne of steam are derived from OIL's generator figures (section 2). Prices are symbols filled from OIL's economics (item 43, registry P-41). Until then O.1–O.3 are used only for the sensitivity check below.

Price sensitivity in the meantime

For the verified candidates, V/day is computed under clearly labelled assumed price scenarios: a base vector, then oil ±30%, diesel ±30%, electricity ±30% and water ×0.5 and ×2. O3 reports two things: whether the knee design stays in the top 3 in every scenario, and the rank agreement across scenarios (Kendall τ). The recommendation is tagged price-robust or price-sensitive. A price-sensitive recommendation names which price decides it.

How provisional limits are treated

Three different pressures are kept apart: the equipment rating (wellhead and tubulars; g1), the historical operating pressure (what OIL has already run; the value g1 uses now, and g2a), and the formation fracture pressure (a rock property; g2b). The historical maximum is never described as, or used as a stand-in for, fracture pressure. A provisional value comes from what OIL has already done safely: its historical operating envelope. It is enforced as a hard limit, because that is the conservative choice, but it is labelled provisional wherever it appears. A design within 5% of a provisional limit is flagged. The optimiser can never relax a provisional limit; only an OIL-confirmed value in the registry can move it. None of these limits replaces the field's own safety systems (relief valves, interlocks).

Robust handling. In screening, objectives use the proxy median and constraints use the conservative quantile (q90 for "≤" limits), so a design passes only if it passes with margin. In verification, each candidate runs on a 16-member subset of the 64-member SE ensemble. The subset is chosen by k-medoids on the standardised values of the upstream parameters that most affect O3's choice (the top-ranked Sobol indices, Part A §13), and each member is weighted by its cluster's share of the 64. With 16 members, "≥ 95%" cannot be resolved, so hard constraints must hold in all 16. The design finally recommended is then re-run on all 64 members and must hold in ≥ 61 of them (95%). The tail metric is the weighted mean of the worst 2 of 16 members (≈ 12.5%), reported as cvar_tail with its tail fraction.

5.6Re-planning during injection

Plans break: generators trip, injectivity falls, VIT underperforms. D6 is a shrinking-horizon re-optimiser: it keeps what has already been injected and re-decides only what remains.

Triggers

  • Delivered sandface heat (T2-A) off plan by > 5% for > 6 h
  • Generator trip or planned stop
  • Wellhead pressure at the limit while the rate is below plan (injectivity falling)
  • Growth or annulus pressure within 10% of a limit
  • Fixed check every 24 h

What it re-decides

  • Remaining steam mass and rate (extend, shorten or hold)
  • Soak ratio for the new injected heat
  • Rate reduction if integrity limits are close

Method: stage A with the current T1-A state as initial condition and the done part fixed, then 4 verification runs. Result in ~2 min, shown as "continue plan" vs "change": expected value either way.

5.7Soak-end advisor

OIL soaks for 50–60% of injection time as a rule. D7 turns soak end into a choice among six opening times, re-made every 6 h with the twin and the pressure fall-off SE is assimilating. It is a plain enumeration: with six options, no optimiser is needed.

End soak now if E[V/day | open now] ≥ E[V/day | open at now + Δ] for all Δ ∈ {6 h, 12 h, 1 d, 2 d, 3 d, 5 d}(O.5) stopping rule
  • Each option is a rollout of the twin at the current build stage over 8 ensemble members, chosen and weighted like the 16-member subset in 5.4 (k = 8): ~6 × 8 short runs every 6 h. O.5 compares weighted means.
  • Soft checks before recommending: the fall-off rate has flattened (most steam condensed, T1-C dVs/dt ≈ 0), and ph is high enough for the flowback O3 assumed (T2-A/T1-C natural-flow check).
  • Season 1 stays within 0.3–0.8 × injection time. Output: "open at [date, time]; +x% value vs the 55% rule; confidence band".

Workflow across a cycle

  1. Trigger: O4 forecasts cut-off within 21 days, or an engineer asks.
  2. Build: D1 reads the as-of state, limits, economics and the tentative O5 window.
  3. Screen, verify, decide (Figure 4). The engineer approves one design or edits and re-verifies it.
  4. Book: D8 sends the request; O5 returns a slot; re-run if the start moved.
  5. Prep: T2-A confirms the ramp-up against growth and annulus limits with the latest completion data.
  6. Inject: D6 watches triggers and re-plans; every change is approved in H.
  7. Soak: D7 recommends the opening time; the engineer confirms.
  8. Learn: after cut-off, compare outcome with the verified bands; feed the cycle to T1-D retraining (Part A 8.4).

Acceptance (proposed; agree with OIL)

  • Every recommended design passes all hard constraints, confirmed and provisional, in all 16 verification members, and in ≥ 61 of 64 members on the final re-run.
  • Each test is reported with the build stage it ran at (5.10). A pass at v0 is a prototype result, not O3 validation.
  • Retrospective: for each historical cycle, O3 is run from that cycle's as-of state. Its recommendation must beat the actual design on the twin by more than the verification P10–P90 spread in at least half the cycles, or O3 is not adding value.
  • Realised cycle value falls inside the verified P10–P90 band in ≥ 70% of pilot cycles.
  • Soak advice: the twin's value at the recommended opening time is higher than at the 55% rule in ≥ 60% of retrospective cycles, and never worse by more than the band.
  • Field pilot: supervised wells vs matched controls on SOR and value per cycle-day (Layer 2 §10).
Guard-rails that do not change with trust

O3 never writes to equipment. Steam decisions always need human approval (Layer 2). Wellhead, annulus and growth limits are hard constraints at their confirmed or provisional value, never soft penalties, and never relaxed by the optimiser. A design outside T1-D's validity envelope cannot be recommended without full-twin verification. When Part A's trust gate is at caution, only designs within the historical operating envelope are shown; at hold, none.
